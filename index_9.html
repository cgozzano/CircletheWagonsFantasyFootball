The Constitution

Article I — Recognition of One Union

All legislative, judicial, and executive powers are vested in the Supreme Commissioner of Fantasy Football Operations, Chris Gozzano. The Circle the Wagons Fantasy Football League has operated as one union since 2013.

Two Leagues, One Union

Since 2024, the league has operated as two distinct-but-equal divisions of eight members each: the Premier League and the Non-Premier League. Each division runs its own draft, position allocation, point system, and playoff structure — independently agreed to by its own participating members.

Promotion & Relegation

At the close of each season, standings determine movement between divisions:

  • The top 4 finishers of the Non-Premier League (per playoff final standings) are promoted to the Premier League for the following year.
  • The bottom 4 finishers of the Premier League are relegated to the Non-Premier League for the following year.

Amended 8/7/2026, by order of the Supreme Commissioner — the original rule promoted/relegated only the top/bottom 2 teams; it now applies to the top/bottom 4.

Contributions & Payouts

The league-wide minimum contribution is $50 USD per member. Lowering the fee requires unanimous (100%) agreement across all participating members; raising it is negotiated within each division.

To honor the standard of play in the top flight, 25% of all Non-Premier League contributions is remitted to the Premier League's payout pot — the remaining 75% stays within the Non-Premier League, distributed per that division's own agreed structure.

Non-Premier: $50 × 8 members = $400 − $100 remitted = $300 pot
Premier: $50 × 8 members = $400 + $100 received = $500 pot

Union-Wide Power Rankings

All 16 teams, ranked 1–16 across both divisions on a single scale.

Each team gets a Power Score built from four weighted components, then all 16 teams are ranked on that single score:

  • Win % (30%) — naturally comparable across leagues since it's just a 0–1 record.
  • Points For (25%) — normalized against your own league's average, so different scoring settings between divisions don't skew the comparison.
  • Point Differential (20%) — also normalized within your own league; rewards blowouts over squeakers.
  • Strength-of-Schedule Score (25%) — each win is credited based on how good that week's opponent was; each loss is penalized based on how bad that opponent was. Beating a 7-1 team counts more than beating a 1-7 team.

This updates automatically as the season progresses and more weeks are played. Early-season rankings will swing more since there's less data to smooth things out.

Forecasted Winners & Losers

Statistical projections for the upcoming week, both divisions.

Important honesty note: Sleeper's public API doesn't expose real player-level projections (injuries, matchup difficulty, weather, etc.) — that data isn't part of their free API. This is a statistical forecast, not a true player-projection model.

For each team, it takes their season scoring average (points for ÷ games played) as their projected score for the upcoming matchup. The projected winner is simply whoever's average is higher for that pairing.

Win probability is calculated from the projected point gap between the two teams, scaled against how consistent scoring has been across their whole division this season — a small gap between two high-variance leagues produces a closer probability than the same gap in a low-variance one.

Treat this as "who the numbers favor," not a guarantee — any given week, a cold streak or a boom game can flip it entirely. It also gets more reliable as more weeks of data pile up; early-season forecasts are closer to a coin flip by nature.

Forecasts will populate once League IDs are configured and at least one week has been played.
